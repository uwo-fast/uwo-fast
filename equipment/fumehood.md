# Fume Hood — Equipment Record

Reference record for the FAST lab chemical fume hood. This is a binder and reference
document, not a posted sign. The wall sign is [Fume Hood](../lab-signs/fumehood.md).

Provenance is separated on purpose. Values read off the installed unit are not the same
as manufacturer catalog values, and neither one is evidence of the hood's current
certified performance. Only the field certification record establishes that.

## Classification

> General-purpose chemical fume hood. FRP-lined. Not a perchloric-acid hood, radioisotope
> hood, biological safety cabinet, chemical-storage enclosure, or primary containment for
> explosive reactions.

## As Installed — Operating Label

The operating-procedure label on the hood states:

- DO NOT use perchloric acid in this fume hood.
- DO NOT use the hood as primary containment for explosive reactions.
- DO NOT use the hood to store chemicals.
- Verify the exhaust system and installed monitoring devices are functioning properly.
- Wear appropriate PPE.
- Open the sash only as far as necessary.
- Use the sash as a face and body shield.
- Keep the sash fully closed when not working.
- Do not place the head or torso inside the enclosure.
- Keep the user's face at least 6 in from the sash.
- If flammable material is present, remove receptacles and ignition sources from within
  the hood enclosure.
- Do not block the bottom airfoil or the baffle openings.
- Minimize the number and size of objects obstructing airflow.
- Keep apparatus and associated work at least 6 in behind the sash.
- Avoid rapid sash, body, arm or hand movements in front of the operating hood.

Mott Pro-series specifications express static-pressure loss of this type in inches of

**These are design reference values. They are not evidence that this hood currently
exhausts 830, 1,035, 533 or 667 CFM.** The actual certified sash height, face velocity,
airflow and alarm setpoint come from the field balancing and certification record.

Mott's manual states its fume hoods are intended to provide containment at roughly
80–120 fpm face velocity, with some models capable of lower operation, and that actual
exhaust requirements depend on sash position.

### Liner and application limits

Model 7321000 is the FRP-lined version. Mott describes FRP as a general-purpose liner
resistant to a broad range of reagents, but states that FRP-lined hoods are not suitable
for perchloric-acid or radioisotope work. Mott also states chemical fume hoods should not
be used for biological work, chemical storage, spray painting, or processes incompatible
with the liner material.

### Supply wiring

Mott's manual specifies 12 AWG minimum supply wiring for a 20 A circuit, permanent
grounding, and a nearby accessible dedicated disconnect or breaker. Hoods not factory
prewired by Mott are to be field-wired to applicable electrical and local codes.

### Setup and airflow practices

- Locate apparatus as far toward the rear as practical, and never closer than 6 in (15 cm)
  from the sash plane.
- Elevate large apparatus approximately 2 in (5 cm) above the work surface where practical,
  to allow airflow underneath.
- Do not overload the hood. Mott gives a rule of thumb of no more than 50% of the available
  work surface covered by equipment or materials.
- Avoid rapid movements at the hood face.
- Avoid pedestrian traffic immediately in front of an operating hood.
- Do not adjust baffles. This is work for qualified airflow or safety personnel.

### Inspection and testing

Before each use:

- Verify exhaust operation.
- Verify the airflow alarm/monitor is operating.
- Check that airflow openings are unobstructed.
- Use the established certified sash working height.

At least annually:

- Containment testing, which Mott recommends before first use and at least annually after.
- Inspect internal sash pulleys and cables for corrosion, fraying or deterioration.
- Inspect the cable attachment to the sash handle.
- Lubricate cables with lithium grease yearly or as required.
- Inspect more frequently under intensive use or in corrosive environments.

If the low-flow alarm activates:

- Close the sash immediately.
- Step away.
- If airflow does not recover and the alarm does not clear, evacuate the affected area and
  contact facilities. An exhaust-system failure may exist.

### Rated environmental conditions

For the hood's electrical equipment:

- Indoor use only
- Pollution Degree 2
- Installation Category II
- Maximum altitude: 3,000 m ASL
- Operating ambient temperature: 5–40 °C
- Maximum RH: 80% through 31 °C, decreasing linearly to 50% RH at 40 °C
